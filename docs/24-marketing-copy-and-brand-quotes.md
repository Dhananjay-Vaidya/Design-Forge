## 2. The "why now" — the case in today's world

> **AI can hand you an answer in seconds. It can't hand you a decision you're able to defend.**

We're surrounded by confident answers — from chatbots, from search, from whoever posts loudest. The
scarce skill is no longer *getting* an opinion. It's weighing the ones you have, knowing why you chose,
and learning whether you chose well.

DecisionForge is built for exactly that gap. The numbers are calculated, transparent, and yours to
audit. AI is on tap when you want a sharper question, a risk you missed, or the case against your
favourite — but it never moves the numbers. You leave with a choice you can explain, and a record you
can learn from.

In a world of cheap answers, a clear method is the advantage.

### 3.2 The problem
**Heading:** Big decisions deserve better than a late-night gut call.
**Body:** Important choices end up scattered across notes, group chats and half-finished spreadsheets.
AI chat gives you a confident paragraph you can't audit a week later. Most "decision tools" stop at a
one-time pros-and-cons list and never check whether you were right. So the same hard decisions get made
the same unreliable way.

### 3.3 Why now (short landing version)
**Heading:** Answers got cheap. Judgment got valuable.
**Body:** Anyone can get an instant opinion now. What's rare is a decision you can stand behind — one
with your priorities made explicit, the trade-offs visible, and a way to learn from how it turned out.
DecisionForge gives you the method, and keeps the AI in an advisory seat where it belongs.

### 3.4 How it works (4 steps)
1. **Frame it.** Capture the decision, your options, and the criteria that actually matter to you.
2. **Score it.** Rate each option against each criterion. DecisionForge computes a transparent,
   weighted ranking.
3. **Pressure-test it.** See why the leader wins, how close the race is, and — if you want — what AI
   thinks you've missed.
4. **Review it.** Commit your choice, then look back later to see whether your confidence matched
   reality.

### 3.5 Benefits grid (6 cards)
- **Transparent by design.** Every ranking shows its working. No black box, no vibes.
- **Works without AI.** The scoring engine is fully yours and fully offline-capable. AI is a bonus,
  never a crutch.
- **AI that stays in its lane.** Risks, assumptions, blind spots, counter-arguments — advice you review,
  not numbers it overwrites.
- **A memory for your decisions.** Snapshots, history, and a calibration profile that shows where you
  tend to be over- or under-confident.
- **Fast to start.** Templates for the decisions people actually face — job offers, rent vs buy, hiring,
  big purchases.
- **Private.** Your decisions are yours. We send the minimum to AI, and never your identity.

### 3.6 Two kinds of output (the core idea)
**Heading:** Two kinds of output, never confused.
**Left card (teal · Calculated):** *The ranking.* Deterministic, reproducible, and yours to audit.
Change an input, see exactly what moves.
**Right card (violet · Advisory):** *The AI insight.* Questions, risks and counter-arguments to sharpen
your thinking — clearly labelled, and powerless over your scores.

### 3.7 Differentiator line
**Heading:** Not a smarter guess. A clearer method.
**Body:** Chatbots optimise for a confident answer. DecisionForge optimises for a decision you can
explain, defend, and revisit. That's the difference between being told what to do and knowing why you
did it.

### 3.8 Trust & privacy strip
**Heading:** Your call. Your data.
**Body:** The scoring engine runs without ever calling out. When you ask for AI, we send only the
decision content it needs — never your email, your account, or anything we don't have to. You can export

### 3.9 Final CTA band

**Sub:** Start free. Bring a real decision. Leave with a choice you can stand behind.

## 4. "Why DecisionForge" page (longer form)

**Page title:** Why DecisionForge
**Lead:** We built DecisionForge because the hardest part of a decision was never the arithmetic. It was
being honest about what matters, seeing the trade-offs clearly, and remembering why you chose.

**Section — The method, not the mood.** A good decision isn't a lucky guess that aged well. It's a
process you could run again and defend to someone who disagrees. DecisionForge makes that process
concrete: name your criteria, weight them honestly, score your options, and let the math be the math.

**Section — AI, kept honest.** AI is genuinely useful for the parts humans are bad at: spotting a blind
spot, naming an uncomfortable assumption, arguing the other side. It's dangerous for the part humans
should own: the final call. So we drew a hard line. AI here can question, suggest, and summarise. It
cannot touch your scores, your weights, or your ranking. Ever.

**Section — Decisions worth remembering.** Every analysis and commitment is snapshotted, so you can look
back at exactly what you knew when you chose. Over time, that becomes a calibration profile — a quiet,
honest mirror showing where your confidence tends to run ahead of reality, and where your instincts are
sharp.

**Section — Who it's for.** The student comparing offers. The professional at a fork. The founder
choosing which bet to make. The person about to spend real money. Anyone whose next choice is worth more
than a coin flip.

**Closing line:** Created by AI, Designed by Human.

## 8. Implementation notes

Implemented in `frontend/src/content/marketing.ts`, `frontend/src/content/dashboard.ts`,
and the public `/why` route. The copy above is the supplied brief; the shipped content
uses the following narrower claims to match the current code:

- Without AI does not mean offline: the public live demo runs in the browser, while
  saved decisions require the backend.
- AI context excludes account identifiers and credentials. Decision text itself can
  contain personal information, so the page does not promise that identity can never
  reach an AI provider.
- AI setup proposals require explicit acceptance; deterministic rankings always use
  the saved inputs. Copy distinguishes advice from approved input changes.
- Snapshots can be saved and commitments capture a snapshot; not every analysis is
  automatically snapshotted.
- The final CTA promises explainable reasoning, rather than a guaranteed right outcome.
- Dashboard Open counts DRAFT and SCORED, Committed includes committed/review states,
  and Review due counts UNDER_REVIEW. If pagination limits the loaded results, the
  dashboard labels the scope instead of claiming an account-wide total.
- House aphorisms are original brand copy, sampled once per dashboard visit. No invented
  testimonials or external quotations are displayed.
